# Speaking Prompt Bank

## A1–A2 — 1 to 5 minutes

1. Introduce yourself without notes.
2. Describe your normal weekday.
3. Describe your home.
4. Describe a family member or friend.
5. Explain what you do for work/study.
6. Describe what you did yesterday.
7. Talk about your next weekend.
8. Explain how to get from your home to a familiar place.
9. Describe food you like and dislike.
10. Compare two places you know.
11. Explain one problem you had recently.
12. Give advice to someone starting your hobby.
13. Talk about a skill you want to learn.
14. Describe a useful piece of technology.
15. Tell a simple story from childhood.

## B1 — 5 to 10 minutes

16. Is remote work better than office work?
17. What makes a good colleague?
18. What is the best way to learn a difficult skill?
19. Should children have smartphones?
20. What makes a city pleasant to live in?
21. Does social media connect or isolate people?
22. Should university focus on theory or practical skills?
23. How can people build better habits?
24. What is one environmental problem in your area?
25. What are the advantages and disadvantages of online shopping?
26. Is failure necessary for learning?
27. What should companies do when employees make mistakes?
28. How has technology changed communication?
29. Is it better to specialise deeply or learn many skills?
30. What makes information trustworthy?

## B2 — 10 to 15 minutes

31. Will AI create more jobs than it destroys?
32. Is privacy becoming a luxury?
33. Should governments regulate recommendation algorithms?
34. What are the hidden costs of convenience?
35. When does automation become a social problem?
36. Should public transport be heavily subsidised?
37. Is economic growth compatible with environmental sustainability?
38. How should society respond to misinformation?
39. Should employers care about degrees or demonstrated skills?
40. What makes a leader legitimate?
41. Should failure be punished in high-risk industries?
42. Does technology make people more productive or merely busier?
43. How should society balance innovation and safety?
44. Is standardised testing a fair way to measure ability?
45. Are experts losing public trust? Why?

## C1 — 15 to 20 minutes

46. To what extent should individuals be responsible for systemic problems?
47. Is technological progress inherently beneficial?
48. How should policymakers act when scientific evidence is uncertain?
49. When does persuasion become manipulation?
50. Is efficiency always a desirable objective?
51. Can a society maximise both freedom and security?
52. Should AI-generated work be treated differently from human-created work?
53. How do incentives distort otherwise rational decisions?
54. Why do organisations repeat known mistakes?
55. What are the limitations of data-driven decision-making?
56. Is inequality primarily an economic, political or cultural problem?
57. What is the role of universities in an age of ubiquitous information?
58. When should experts defer to public preferences?
59. Can regulation keep pace with technological change?
60. What does it mean to be well educated in the twenty-first century?

## Recording loop

For any prompt:

1. Speak once with no notes.
2. Transcribe 60–120 seconds of your own speech.
3. Mark fillers, pauses, grammar errors and repeated basic words.
4. Build a 5-item repair list.
5. Speak again using only five keyword notes.
6. Compare recordings.
