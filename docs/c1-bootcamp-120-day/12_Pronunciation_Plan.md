# Pronunciation & Listening Mechanics Plan

Pronunciation is trained for **intelligibility and listening gains**, not accent imitation.

## Daily 15-minute core

1. 3 min — mouth warm-up and troublesome sounds.
2. 4 min — word stress: say 10 target words.
3. 4 min — sentence stress / rhythm: shadow 4–6 lines.
4. 4 min — linking/reduction: repeat one short natural sentence 10 times.

## Days 1–14 — make basic sounds stable

Focus on contrasts that commonly block intelligibility:

- final consonants: work / worked; want / wants
- /s/ vs /ʃ/: see / she
- /tʃ/ vs /ʃ/: chip / ship
- /r/ vs /l/ if difficult
- /θ/ and /ð/: think / this
- long vs short vowels: ship/sheep, full/fool, live/leave
- plural and third-person endings
- -ed endings: /t/, /d/, /ɪd/

Do not attempt all sounds every day. Pick one contrast and collect real examples.

## Days 15–35 — stress and rhythm

Train:

- stressed content words
- reduced function words
- schwa in unstressed syllables
- contractions
- strong vs weak forms

Example principle:

> I **WANT** to **FINISH** the **WORK** by **FRIDAY**.

The grammatical words are usually lighter than the content words.

## Days 36–70 — connected speech

Notice and imitate:

- consonant-to-vowel linking
- consonant-to-consonant simplification
- /t/ and /d/ changes in fast speech
- “going to”, “want to”, “have to” in natural speech
- thought groups / breath groups

Use YouGlish to compare several real speakers rather than copying a single clip.

## Days 71–100 — prosody for meaning

Train:

- contrastive stress
- certainty vs uncertainty
- agreement/disagreement tone
- list intonation
- signalling the end vs continuation of an idea

Record the same sentence with two intended meanings and listen back.

## Days 101–120 — C1 delivery

Focus on:

- stable pace under cognitive pressure
- strategic pausing rather than fillers
- highlighting key claims
- sounding engaged during interaction
- correcting yourself without breaking fluency

## Filler replacement ladder

Instead of repeated “uh… um…”, train short planning chunks:

- Let me think for a moment.
- The way I see it, …
- There are two points I’d make here.
- What matters most is …
- I’d distinguish between X and Y.
- That depends largely on …

Use these sparingly; C1 does not mean filling every pause with a phrase.

## Weekly recording comparison

Every Day 7:

1. select 60 seconds from today’s recording;
2. compare it with the previous week;
3. score: intelligibility, stress, rhythm, linking, pace, fillers;
4. choose **one** pronunciation target for the next week.
